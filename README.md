## A note on how I'm sequencing this

I used to plan this as one long straight line — finish *all* DSA, then *all* CS fundamentals, then finally touch backend dev. I've since decided that's a mistake, for two reasons:

1. **Backend dev shouldn't be a total surprise later.** Once I've got SQL basics down, I'm planning to build one tiny, ugly Spring Boot + PostgreSQL CRUD app — nothing impressive, just enough that Spring Boot isn't something I'm seeing for the first time months from now under pressure.
2. **Docker makes more sense attached to a project I understand**, not as an abstract topic I cram in right before placement season. So I'm pulling that forward too, once I have something worth containerizing.

Also: DSA doesn't get to become a "supporting activity" once I hit backend/CS fundamentals. I'm keeping a floor of at least 5 problems a week no matter what phase I'm in, so it doesn't quietly stall out for months.

## The actual goal

Not "finish the roadmap." The goal is to walk into interviews and:

- Actually understand the DSA I'm using, not just recognize the pattern from a video
- Explain my projects' design decisions without stumbling
- Be comfortable with SQL/DBMS deep enough for a fintech interview, not just surface-level
- Have 2 projects I'm genuinely proud of instead of 6 shallow tutorial clones

Everything above is in service of that, not the other way around.
